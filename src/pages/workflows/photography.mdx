# Photography

Photography is where a shoot becomes a live storefront image. You name your files by SKU, point ThreadCloud at the folder, and it uploads each photo to Shopify, names it for search engines, writes alt text, and attaches it to the right product. Two more cards on the same page tell you how much of the storefront still has no image and which products to shoot next.

<div class="callout callout-info">
<div class="callout-title">Last verified against the app: 2026-10-01</div>
If a button name here doesn't match what you see, the app changed and this page is stale. Tell the team.
</div>

<div class="callout callout-info">
<div class="callout-title">When to use</div>
After a shoot, once the products already exist in Shopify. Photography comes <strong>after</strong> the push: a photo can only land on a product that's already in the store. Photos from the buying appointment belong on the order instead (see <a href="/workflows/build-order/">Parse & Build</a>).
</div>

## What you'll need

- A folder of finished shots, each named by the product's SKU (see below)
- The products already **pushed to Shopify** (Photography matches a file to a product by SKU)
- Files as **JPEG or PNG**. iPhone HEIC files are refused: export them as JPEG first.

## Name the files by SKU

ThreadCloud finds the right product from the filename. Name each shot `SKU_1.jpg`, `SKU_2.jpg`, `SKU_3.jpg`, and so on:

- The number after the underscore is the shot order.
- The **lowest number is the hero**. Shopify features the first image on a product, so `_1` becomes the main storefront photo.
- The extension can be `.jpg`, `.jpeg` or `.png`.

<div class="callout callout-tip">
<div class="callout-title">One product, several angles</div>
`ABC123_1.jpg`, `ABC123_2.jpg`, `ABC123_3.jpg` all attach to the same product, hero first. Shoot as many angles as you like. Keep the base SKU identical and bump the number.
</div>

## The photography flow

<ol class="steps">
  <li><strong>Open Photography</strong> from the menu.</li>
  <li><strong>Choose shoot folder</strong>: the whole folder of SKU-named images.</li>
  <li><strong>Sync to Shopify</strong>: files upload one at a time, hero first.</li>
  <li><strong>Watch the status chips</strong>: Pushed, Skipped, No match, Failed.</li>
  <li><strong>Check coverage</strong>: the percentage and what's still missing.</li>
  <li><strong>Work the shoot list</strong>: the exact products with no photo.</li>
</ol>

## Stage 1: Sync a shoot folder

In the **Sync photos** card, click <strong>Choose shoot folder</strong> and pick the folder. ThreadCloud reads the image files only and shows the count it's about to sync.

Click <strong>Sync to Shopify</strong>. Files go up **one at a time**, sorted so each product's hero lands first. A <strong>Stop</strong> button halts the run mid-folder. Anything already synced stays synced.

For each file, ThreadCloud:

1. Finds the Shopify product from the SKU in the filename.
2. Cleans the image: turns it the right way up, caps its size, strips camera data, and saves it as JPEG.
3. Works out what kind of shot it is (front, back, side, detail, flat lay, on model).
4. Builds a search-friendly filename (`brand-title-colour-NN.jpg`) and **alt text** from the product's brand, title and colour plus that shot type.
5. Uploads it to Shopify and attaches it to the product.

Each file gets a status chip:

- <span class="badge badge-ok">PUSHED</span>: attached to the product
- <span class="badge badge-info">SKIPPED</span>: already synced before (safe to re-run a folder)
- <span class="badge badge-warn">NO MATCH</span>: no Shopify product for that SKU yet
- <span class="badge badge-danger">FAILED</span>: the file couldn't be read or uploaded

The bar at the top reads *Hanging the shoot* while it runs and *Photos hung* when it's done (*Nothing new to hang* if every file was already there).

<div class="callout callout-tip">
<div class="callout-title">Re-running a folder is safe</div>
ThreadCloud remembers every file it has pushed, by SKU and filename. Sync the same folder twice and the done ones skip. Add a couple of new angles later and re-sync the whole folder without fear of duplicates.
</div>

## Stage 2: Check storefront coverage

The **Storefront photo coverage** card answers one question: how much of what's for sale has a picture?

The scan runs by itself when you open the page and the last one is more than an hour old (Owners and buyers). You can click <strong>Re-scan storefront</strong> any time. ThreadCloud reads your **active, in-stock** Shopify products and counts how many show a customer-facing image, then gives you a big percentage, how many still need shooting, and how many are not for the shoot.

<div class="callout callout-warn">
<div class="callout-title">Coverage is the storefront</div>
This counts the image a shopper sees on Shopify. A product can have photos inside ThreadCloud and still show nothing online. That still counts as a gap.
</div>

On purpose:

- **Draft, archived and zero-stock products are left out.** The list is what's actually for sale.
- **Products that will never be shot are left out too**: gift cards, anything with nothing to ship (alterations, services, deposits), and products whose type or tags say made to measure, MTM, custom, bespoke, alteration, service or tailoring. They show only in the "not for the shoot" count.
- If you re-scan too soon, it keeps the last good number and shows a soft warning rather than wiping it.

## Stage 3: Work the shoot list

Below coverage, the **Shoot list** card turns the gap into a to-do list: brands with products missing photos, newest arrivals first. Inside a brand, products are ordered by the date they were first received, so the latest delivery is on top.

- **Sort** switches between **Newest arrivals** and **Most to shoot**. **Last 30 days** narrows the list to what arrived this month.
- Click a **brand name** to open that brand's products in Shopify admin.
- Click the **row** to open the exact products with no image: title, colour, SKU, season, first-received date and price, each linking to the product in Shopify admin.

### Pull the products with the pick list

Press <strong>Pick list</strong> to turn the list into a floor view for your phone: one big row per product under its brand. Tap a product as you pull it from the rack. It greys out and drops to the bottom of its brand, and the count at the top goes down. Picks stay on that phone until the product is shot or you press <strong>Clear picks</strong>, so you can reload or come back later. <strong>Print</strong> prints only the list, with a box beside each product. <strong>Back to shoot list</strong> returns to the full card.

Shoot those, re-sync the folder, and re-scan. The percentage climbs and the products drop off the list.

<div class="callout callout-info">
<div class="callout-title">The list is capped for focus</div>
Each brand shows up to 60 missing products at a time, with a "+N more" note. Clear the visible batch, re-scan, and the next ones show up. Older products with no ThreadCloud record show fewer details; the SKU and Shopify link still work.
</div>

## Studio access (Owner only)

If you shoot through **Straight Goods Studio** instead of syncing a folder by hand, the Owner connects the two in the **Connect to Straight Goods Studio** card:

1. Click <strong>Grant access</strong>. ThreadCloud makes a token for your store that can only push product images.
2. **Copy the token now.** It's shown **once**. Paste it into the Studio.
3. <strong>Rotate token</strong> makes a new one and stops the old one. <strong>Revoke</strong> turns access off.

From then on the Studio pushes finished shots straight to Shopify the same way the folder sync does: same SKU matching, same filenames and alt text.

<div class="callout callout-warn">
<div class="callout-title">Who can do what</div>
Owners and buyers can sync folders and scan coverage. Granting, rotating and revoking Studio access is Owner only. Viewers see an explainer.
</div>

## Common mistakes

<div class="callout callout-warn">
<div class="callout-title">Shooting before the product is in Shopify</div>
A SKU with no Shopify product yet comes back <span class="badge badge-warn">NO MATCH</span>. Nothing breaks, but nothing lands either. Push first, then sync.
</div>

<div class="callout callout-warn">
<div class="callout-title">Files not named by SKU</div>
A file without a SKU in its name can't be matched. Keep the name `SKU_1.jpg`: the base is the exact product SKU, the number is the order.
</div>

<div class="callout callout-warn">
<div class="callout-title">HEIC straight off the phone</div>
iPhone HEIC files are refused. Export them as JPEG, then re-sync. The rest of the folder goes through either way.
</div>
