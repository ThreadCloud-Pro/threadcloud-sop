# Custom orders

Custom orders are one-off special orders: a made-to-measure suit, a special-order jacket, a monogrammed tie, anything you buy for one customer (or a few customers on one maker's order). You start from the maker's confirmation, review the details, send the products to Shopify so they can be scanned at the register, and receive and tag them when they land.

<div class="callout callout-info">
<div class="callout-title">Last verified against the app: 2026-11-17</div>
This screen is labelled <strong>Custom orders</strong> in the app (it used to be called MTM). If a button name here doesn't match, the app changed and this page is stale. Tell the team.
</div>

## Quick path

<ol class="steps">
  <li><strong>New custom order</strong>: drop in the maker's confirmation, or enter it by hand.</li>
  <li><strong>Review</strong>: the customer, the garments, the measurements, the price and the delivery date.</li>
  <li><strong>SEND TO SHOPIFY</strong>: creates the products, ready to scan at the register.</li>
  <li><strong>Mark received</strong> when the garments arrive, and enter the final cost once for each piece or suit. You can also do this from the phone's <strong>Receive</strong> tab.</li>
  <li><strong>Print the labels</strong>, tag the garments, and ring them up on POS by scanning each tag when the customer collects.</li>
  <li>The order closes itself, piece by piece, as Shopify shows the garments handed over. If a piece never went through a sale, mark it <strong>picked up</strong> by hand.</li>
</ol>

Every custom order page shows one step bar: **Review**, **Ordered**, **Received**. **Fitted** joins the bar only once the order has been picked up. Ordered means it has gone to Shopify and the maker, and is waiting to arrive.

## Start a custom order

On the home screen, switch to the **Sales floor** desk and click the <strong>Custom order</strong> card ("Made to measure, special order"). It opens the **Custom orders** list. You can also reach it from Home's <strong>Receive custom orders</strong>, or from **Logistics**, <strong>MTM Orders</strong>.

The list has a search box ("Customer, maker or garment") and tabs: **All open**, **Draft**, **Ordered**, **Received**, **Fitted** and **Cancelled**. Each row shows the **Customer**, **Maker**, **Garment**, when it was **Ordered**, when it is **Expected** (late ones say so) and a status chip. Click a row to open the order.

Click <strong>New custom order</strong>. Two ways in:

- **Drop the supplier confirmation**: a PDF, an image or a text file. ThreadCloud reads it and pulls out the customer, the maker and reference, the garments and options, the price, and any measurements it can see. This is the fast path when the maker sent you a confirmation.
- <strong>Enter the order manually</strong> ("No confirmation to upload?"): for a phoned-in order. It skips the upload and opens a blank order.

Either way you land on the review screen with whatever was found filled in.

<div class="callout callout-tip">
<div class="callout-title">One order, several confirmations</div>
If an order came as more than one confirmation (say a jacket from one maker and trousers from another), drop all the files in at once. ThreadCloud reads each one and builds a single order from them. If one file can't be read, the rest still go through; fill that garment in by hand.
</div>

## Review

Everything on the review screen **saves on its own** as you go. Watch for **Saving…** then **Saved**. Work top to bottom:

### A · Customer

Type a name, or search your Shopify customers and tap the right one ("Is this them? Tap to confirm."). When the maker's document names a customer, ThreadCloud says whether the customer on the order matches it: **Matches the document**, **Likely match** or **Different name**. With no customer chosen yet, it looks the document's name up in your Shopify customers. One exact match is chosen for you. A close one (a short form of the first name, an initial, a different middle name) is offered with the reason and a **Use** button, never chosen silently. If they're new, use <strong>+ CREATE</strong> to make them in Shopify. A customer isn't required to send the order: an unnamed order can be attached to the customer at the register.

For a trunk show or a split order, click <strong>DIFFERENT CLIENTS PER ITEM</strong> to give each garment its own client. <strong>BACK TO ONE CLIENT</strong> undoes it.

### B · Garments

One card per garment or product. For each one:

- **Choose product type**. Every garment needs one before you can send.
- **Size ordered**. It prints on the tag ("Add the size · prints on the tag"). **Size scale** sets the scale the size is read on and printed under; change it if the size reads wrong.
- **Fabric**, with an optional fabric photo, and details such as lapel, lining and buttons. <strong>+ ADD FIELD</strong> adds another detail.
- **Adjustments**, **Qty**, notes for the item, detail photos and a spec sheet.
- **COST** (what you pay the maker) and **RETAIL** (what the customer pays). When the maker's confirmation printed no amount for a piece, the price shows a quiet "No amount printed" and you type it in.

Click <strong>ADD GARMENT</strong> for more. Custom orders aren't limited to tailoring: ties, scarves and accessories work too.

#### Bill several pieces as one

Pieces sold together don't have to be priced one by one. Tick two or more garment cards and click <strong>Combine N · bill as one</strong>, or click <strong>All one item</strong> to group the whole order. A jacket with trousers becomes a **Suit**; any other mix (a shirt and tie, two shirts) becomes a **Set**. A group has one cost and one retail price, and goes to Shopify as one product. <strong>Ungroup</strong> gives each piece its own price back. An order can hold several groups alongside single pieces.

### C · Fit profile

The customer's measurements. Toggle **IN** or **CM**. Only the measurements that matter for the garment type show. When the customer is linked in Shopify, their measurements are saved to their profile; only the fields you fill in update it, blanks leave what's there alone.

### D · Pricing & delivery

- **Customer total**: the sum of the garment prices. It fills in once every garment has a price.
- **Maker currency**: what you pay the maker in.
- **Deposit** (optional): what the customer paid up front, in your store currency.
- **Estimated delivery**: required.

The meter beside the heading counts down what's left ("2 of 4 to send") and reads **Ready to send** when the product type, the garment prices, the currency and the delivery date are all in.

### E · Supplier confirmation

The **Brand / maker** and the maker's **Reference number**.

## Send to Shopify

When the meter reads **Ready to send**, click <strong>SEND TO SHOPIFY</strong>. It reads **CREATING PRODUCTS…** while it works. ThreadCloud creates a product in Shopify for each garment (or each group), with a barcode, ready to scan at the register. The order moves to **Ordered** on the step bar and waits for delivery.

If the button is greyed out, hover over it to see what's missing. If Shopify refuses, the screen says "Couldn't send to Shopify." with the reason.

<div class="callout callout-warn">
<div class="callout-title">Walk the customer through the spec first</div>
Custom orders are non-returnable. Go over the spec with the customer, and take a deposit, before the order goes to the maker. Once it's with the maker, changes are expensive or impossible.
</div>

## Receive and tag

An Ordered order opens on its summary: a headline such as "Arriving tomorrow.", or "Expected Oct 2." with "3 days late" beside it, the customer, garment, measurements, maker, supplier reference, expected date, price and notes.

When the garments land, work through **Check each tag** ("Match each tag's code, size and price to its row. Anything off, fix it in Review first."). For each garment, find its row by the **Tag** code, then check the **Size** and the **Price** match the tag.

Then enter the final **Cost**, the **Customer total** if it changed, and any **Notes** (for example "fabric upgrade billed extra"), and click <strong>Mark received</strong> in the dark bar at the bottom. ThreadCloud books the stock into Shopify and tells you if it couldn't.

**Enter a suit's cost once.** The maker bills a suit as one amount, so the receipt has one field for the whole suit, labelled with the suit, its pieces and the customer (for example **Suit cost**), on the suit's first row. The other pieces of that suit read **In suit** and take no cost of their own. That one figure settles the suit and goes to its one Shopify product. A single piece, such as a shirt or a tie, still has its own **Cost**. If a suit was earlier costed piece by piece and not completely, the receipt says so and asks for the suit's cost once.

Once received, **What came in** shows the **Final cost**, the **Retail price** and the **Margin**. The figure is labelled **Retail price** because it is the price, not a claim that it was paid: a **Paid**, **Partly paid**, **Unpaid** or **Refunded** mark appears beside it only when the order is linked to a Shopify sale that says so. With no linked sale it reads **No Shopify order linked**. The dark bar offers <strong>Print labels</strong>: one label per tag on the Dymo. The **…** menu in the header has:

- <strong>Print labels on a sheet</strong>: a sheet of labels for a regular office printer.
- <strong>Receiving document</strong>: garments, units and cost.
- <strong>Undo receipt</strong>: sends the order back to Ordered. A confirm asks first ("Undo this receipt?") and says that costs, customer total and receipt notes are cleared. Click <strong>Undo receipt</strong> to go ahead, or <strong>Keep it</strong>. It's offered only until the order has sold.
- <strong>Delete order</strong>.

<strong>Edit receipt</strong>, under What came in, lets you correct the cost, total or notes later (<strong>Save changes</strong>).

## On the sales floor (POS)

There is no special tile or cart for custom orders. When **SEND TO SHOPIFY** runs, each piece (or each suit, which is one product) becomes a Shopify product that sells only at the register, never online. Its SKU is the order's code and the piece's number, the same code printed on its label.

When the customer collects, **scan each tag at the register** and ring it up like any other sale. Add the customer to the cart in POS before you take payment: Shopify credits the salesperson and the customer natively. No draft order is created.

ThreadCloud notices the sale (Shopify tells it) and links it to the custom order when the sale carries every piece of the order. A sale that carries only some of the pieces does not link. The order page then shows **Paid**, **Unpaid** and the other payment marks from that sale.

<div class="callout callout-tip">
<div class="callout-title">Collected in two visits</div>
If the customer takes some pieces now and the rest later, ring up each piece when it leaves. Each piece closes when its own sale is fulfilled in Shopify.
</div>

## Receive on the phone

The phone's **Receive** tab also receives custom orders, so the garments can be checked in at the rack.

<ol class="steps">
  <li>Open <strong>Receive</strong>, type the order's number, the customer or the brand in <strong>Find an order</strong>, and tap <strong>Find</strong>. Custom orders that have been ordered from the maker and not yet received show in a <strong>Custom orders</strong> group, marked <strong>Custom</strong>, beside any buying orders the search finds.</li>
  <li>Open the order. Under <strong>Pieces</strong>, each suit is one row and identical pieces are grouped on one row (shown as ×2, for example), each with its code and how many photos it has.</li>
  <li>Tap the camera button on a piece. The in-app camera names each shot it wants (such as front, label and inside), and you can also pick a photo from the phone's library. The photos are kept with the order as received photos, named for the piece, and show beside the maker's confirmation on the desk.</li>
  <li>Tap <strong>Mark received</strong>. It is the same receipt the desk makes. There is no whole-order shortcut for photos: shoot the pieces you want first.</li>
</ol>

The phone takes no cost. Enter the suit or piece cost on the desk with **Edit receipt** (see above). An order holds at most a set number of photos, and the phone says **This order already holds as many photos as it can.** when it is full. If Shopify cannot be reached from the phone, it says so and asks you to finish on the desktop.

## Picked up and closed

A custom order stays open until the customer has taken every piece home. Open orders show on the list's **All open**, **Ordered** and **Received** tabs and nag on Home. A closed order moves to the **Fitted** tab, where it stays findable, and leaves Home.

**Shopify closes it for you.** When you ring the garment up at POS and the sale is fulfilled in Shopify, ThreadCloud notices (Shopify tells it) and stamps that piece **Picked up**. A suit or other group billed as one counts as one piece. A piece on a sale is closed by that sale, not by hand. The order page shows **Picked up** with the date, or "2 of 3" while some pieces are still in the store. When every piece is out, ThreadCloud says so in the strip at the bottom and returns you to Home.

**By hand, only when there is no sale.** In the **…** menu on a received order:

- <strong>Mark picked up</strong> closes an order that no Shopify sale carries (the customer collected it and nobody rang it up). On an order of several pieces the menu also offers <strong>Mark [piece] picked up</strong> for each piece, and **Mark all picked up** for the rest.
- While a piece sits on a sale Shopify doesn't yet show as handed over, the menu offers nothing: the sale's fulfilment will close it. If the fulfilment never closes it, the button comes back once Shopify reads the sale as handed over and the stock has settled.

**Cancel and reopen.**

- <strong>Cancel order</strong> is on an **Ordered** order that has no Shopify draft order or POS sale yet. It asks first ("Cancel this custom order? It is kept for the record, and Reopen brings it back.").
- <strong>Reopen order</strong> is on a picked-up order (it goes back to **Received**) or a cancelled one (back to **Ordered**, or **Review** if it never went to the maker).

The list's search and Home's search also find a custom order by its code (for example MTM-0113) or just its number (113).

Each garment also links to its Shopify product from the order page, and products pushed for a custom order carry the maker's reference and the confirmation colour. Clean Catalog leaves custom order products alone.

## Fit notes

Fit notes after pickup are not built yet. For now, keep alteration notes in the receipt's notes or on the customer's record.

## Owner tools on the Custom orders list

Only the store's account owner has a <strong>More</strong> menu in the list's header. Floor, Receiver, Buyer and Manager don't see it:

- <strong>Barcode check</strong>: lists custom-order products that share a barcode or have no order behind them. It changes nothing until you choose to archive the stray copies.
- <strong>Brand check</strong>: a read-only check of which pushed custom orders lack a brand or sell under another vendor. It reads **Every custom order is on its brand** when all is well, otherwise how many are to fix and how many need a brand chosen.

## If it goes wrong

- **The upload didn't read the confirmation well**: fix the fields on review, or start over with <strong>Enter the order manually</strong>.
- **SEND TO SHOPIFY is greyed out**: every garment needs a product type and a price, and the order needs a currency and a delivery date. Hover over the button to see which is missing.
- **Anything else**: see <a href="/runbook/">If something breaks</a>.
