# Catalog setup

Catalog setup brings the products already in your Shopify store up to ThreadCloud's standard: category, colour, size, pattern, season and stock type. Once published, they show up in your storefront filters and in Merchandising. The page is called **Clean Catalog**.

<div class="callout callout-info">
<div class="callout-title">Last verified against the app: 2026-10-12</div>
If a tab or button here doesn't match the app, it changed and this page is stale. Tell the team.
</div>

<div class="callout callout-info">
<div class="callout-title">When to use</div>
ThreadCloud labels your catalog during <a href="/setup/onboarding/">first-time setup</a>, so most stores only come here to review what it left for a person, to scan products added since, or when Home shows a <strong>Catalog enrichment</strong> banner.
</div>

## Where it is

- **Settings** → the **Clean Catalog** card → **Open Clean Catalog**.
- **Settings → Automations** → the **Catalog** row → **Refine catalog**.
- Home's **Catalog enrichment** banner or Needs you row.

## What it never changes

Publishing adds ThreadCloud's category, colour, pattern and size facts to your Shopify products. **Titles, prices, photos, inventory, and existing variants stay unchanged.** Anything ThreadCloud isn't sure of is held for you to review, not written.

## The page at a glance

From the top:

- **The status line**, for example "3,868 published · 71 ready · 263 to review · 837 to try again".
- **One button for the next step**. It changes with where your catalog stands:

| What it shows | When |
|---|---|
| **Scan my products** | Nothing scanned yet |
| **Scanning 1,240 of 5,110** (no button) | ThreadCloud is working through your products. You can leave the page; it carries on. |
| **Review 263 products** | Some products are waiting for you |
| **Publish 71 products** | Review is done and products are ready (account owner) |
| **Try 837 again** | Some products did not finish |
| **Check products** | Products were read but checking stopped |
| **Everything is published** (no button) | Nothing is waiting. If you dismissed some products, it says how many. |

- **Decide once per brand**, when a brand has products waiting for a stock type.
- **The review table**, with the tabs **To review**, **Ready** and **Published**.
- **Problems**, only when some products did not finish.
- **Danger zone**, closed, at the bottom (account owner).

## First run

<ol class="steps">
  <li>Click <strong>Scan my products</strong>. ThreadCloud checks your active Shopify products for category, colour and size. You can leave the page; it carries on.</li>
  <li>When the check finishes, the status line says how many are ready and how many are waiting for you.</li>
  <li>Review the products waiting for you (below).</li>
  <li>Publish the ready ones (below).</li>
</ol>

## Review product decisions

<ol class="steps">
  <li>Click <strong>Review N products</strong>, or open the <strong>To review</strong> tab.</li>
  <li>Narrow it with <strong>Brand</strong>, or with <strong>Fix together</strong>, which groups products missing the same thing.</li>
  <li>Click a row to open it. Check <strong>Category</strong>, <strong>Subcategory</strong>, <strong>Colour Group</strong>, <strong>Colour Name</strong>, <strong>Pattern</strong>, <strong>Size Scale</strong>, <strong>Season</strong>, <strong>Stock Type</strong> and <strong>Vendor Ref</strong>.</li>
  <li>If ThreadCloud's suggestions are right, click <strong>Keep these suggestions</strong>. If not, correct the fields and click <strong>Save product details</strong>.</li>
</ol>

<div class="callout callout-tip">
<div class="callout-title">"To review" doesn't mean wrong</div>
It means a fact is missing or ThreadCloud isn't sure. Often the suggestion is right and one click keeps it.
</div>

For genuinely old product with no meaningful season, type **LEGACY** in the Season field.

### Many products at once

- Tick rows (shift-click selects a range on the page), then **Edit selected products**. Pick each field to change, or **Don't change**, then **Apply changes to N products**.
- **Dismiss** in the same dialog drops low-quality rows from the review queue without writing anything to Shopify.
- **Choose catalog family** then **Group selected** sets Menswear or Womenswear for the selected products.
- Changing the brand or the tab clears your selection, so a bulk action never includes rows you can't see.

### Decide once per brand

Most brands are bought one way. The **Decide once per brand** card lists each brand with how its reviewed products split. Pick a **Set stock type** for the brand (**Leave for review**, **MTO**, **Stock**, **NOS**, **Special**) and click **Apply to N products**. Where a brand's history is lopsided, the likely type is already selected. It never overwrites a stock type you set by hand.

## Publish to Shopify

Only the store's account owner can publish.

<ol class="steps">
  <li>Click <strong>Publish N products</strong> to publish every ready product. Or open the <strong>Ready</strong> tab, tick the products you want, and click <strong>Publish selected (N)</strong>.</li>
  <li>Confirm.</li>
</ol>

Published products move to the **Published** tab and show up in your storefront filters.

## Problems

When some products did not finish, a **Problems** section names how many, for example "837 products did not finish". It usually means Shopify was unavailable while they were being checked. If any of them may already be partly written to Shopify, it says so.

- **Try again** checks them again. If a catalog repair is still running, it says so; try again when it finishes.
- **Dismiss** takes them off the list without writing anything to Shopify. Products that may be partly written are kept, so they can still be fixed or undone.
- **See the products** opens the **Problems** tab, with the reason on each product. A product repair from Reorder that failed shows **Retry repair** there.

## Undo a publish

Only the store's account owner sees the **Danger zone**. Changes there are written to your Shopify store.

<ol class="steps">
  <li>Open <strong>Danger zone</strong> at the bottom of the page.</li>
  <li>Tick the products to undo, then <strong>Undo a publish</strong>.</li>
  <li>Type the number of products to confirm, then click <strong>Undo N products</strong>. ThreadCloud puts back the product facts your store had before they were published.</li>
</ol>

## ThreadCloud staff tools

Below the review table, ThreadCloud staff see tools for running each stage by hand, resetting or importing the catalog, and checking footwear sizing. Stores don't see them. If something here needs one, ask the team.

## Catalog profile

**Settings → Catalog &amp; content**, the **Vocabulary** card, <strong>Catalog profile</strong> ("What the store's Shopify catalog holds today"). It counts what ThreadCloud has read from your catalog. It only counts; nothing on it changes Shopify.

| Section | What it counts |
|---|---|
| **Products** | Products read from Shopify, split into **Merchandise**, **Made to order** (MTM, bespoke, special order) and **Not merchandise** (gift cards, alterations, deposits), plus products **Made in ThreadCloud** |
| **Descriptions** | How many products have a description, and about how many words |
| **Brands** | How many vendors |
| **Basic pass** | **Ready for the storefront** and **Need a look**, with what is missing on the ones that need a look (for example category, garment type, size scale, colour, or store codes) |
| **Product types** and **Most used tags** | The store's own types and tags, most used first |

The basic pass runs on its own after each catalog scan. It works out the facts it can prove from what Shopify already holds and marks each product **ready** or **needs a look**. It never guesses, and it writes nothing to Shopify. If the page says ThreadCloud hasn't read the catalog yet, scan products from Clean Catalog first.

## Store codes

**Settings → Catalog &amp; content**, the **Vocabulary** card, <strong>Store codes</strong> ("What the store's class codes and delivery windows mean"). Many stores tag products with their own codes. Say once what they mean, and the basic pass reads them on every product that carries them.

The page lists any **Code families in this catalog** it found in your tags, with examples. Map each one below, or list it to ignore. Four boxes, one entry per line:

| Box | What to type, for example |
|---|---|
| **Class codes** | A code and the category it means, with the garment type if you know it: `501 = Knitwear / Crewneck`. A code ending in `*` covers a series: `7* = Neckwear`. |
| **Knit weight codes** | Tags that mean a knit is light or heavy: `501 = Lightweight` |
| **Delivery windows** | Each delivery window and the season it ships in (FW, SS, PF or CR): `D1 = SS` |
| **Tag families to ignore** | Code families that mean nothing to the catalog, such as a supplier's style codes |

Click <strong>Save codes</strong>. It reads "Saved. The basic pass is running again." Only the products a change affects are looked at again. A class code pointing at a category the catalog doesn't have is refused and named. Only the account owner can save; others can read the page.

## Common mistakes

<div class="callout callout-warn">
<div class="callout-title">Publishing without reviewing</div>
Publish only what's on the Ready tab. Walk To review at least once; old catalog data is thinner than a supplier linesheet.
</div>
